## Why the larger comparison stopped

The frozen inventory has seven eligible Luna sessions, which reduce to three
readable task families plus two encrypted assignments. Two pairs of sessions
continue the same original tasks. All readable families are in one project. Of
those, only the HUD removal is currently an accepted routine implementation example;
the other two concern live acceptance. Nine additional independent routine Luna
cases cannot be recovered from this snapshot. Assessing nine more Sol cases would
not fix that imbalance.

The existing Sol implementation example involves several authoring surfaces; its
comparability to a single HUD removal is unverified. Recorded worker usage also
varies substantially: those two session snapshots contain about 16.60M input/85.7k
output for the Luna example and 3.41M input/15.1k output for the Sol example. Most
input is cached. These different tasks and unallocated session counters **do not
establish a model cost ratio**; they demonstrate why nominal model price alone is
insufficient. Both examples contain observed workflow or development repairs.

## Readiness rule

[Routing evidence readiness](../../skills/model-routing/references/routing-evidence.md)
defines checkpoints: 1–4 independent tasks support examples, 5–9 early signals,
10–20 permit evaluating a provisional preference, and 30–50 may support stronger
estimates. Counts alone never establish confidence.

A provisional preference also requires comparable difficulty/tools, several task
families and projects, audited judgments, missingness sensitivity, uncertainty and
family sensitivity, attributable cost, and fresh validation. The proposed routine
comparison margins are at most 10 percentage points of quality loss and at least
20% resource saving, supported by 95% uncertainty bounds. These are experiment
proposals, not an approved automatic quality trade-off. The statistical estimator
and automatic calibration remain unimplemented.
