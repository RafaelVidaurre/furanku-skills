# Model-routing criteria audit

Research date: 2026-09-24. Sources are the repository and the cited first-party documentation or benchmark providers. This is a read-only audit of the current selector, not a new model ranking.

## What currently decides a route

The public catalog has **11 launch offers** (some are the same model and effort through different agents). It records five capability dimensions: reasoning, implementation, agentic work, UI, and spatial/3D. A known cell has a score, a documented lower `conservative` estimate, confidence, date, evidence, and scale; unknown cells state why. Reasoning uses Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2, agentic uses mapped AA-Briefcase v1.1 Elo, and implementation mixes CursorBench 4.0 with explicitly separate DeepSWE v1.1 scales. UI and spatial coverage remains sparse. The catalog also holds features, context, economics, routes, and a built-in 3D routing preference. [Catalog](../../skills/model-routing/references/routing-catalog.json); [normalization research](model-routing-score-normalization-2026-09-22.md); [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking); [CursorBench](https://cursor.com/evals); [DeepSWE](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/).

Local code validates candidates and filters disabled/explicit offers, launch surface, required features/context, quota/authentication, and maximum-effort basis **before** Jev sees options. Exact principal-requested routes bypass ordinary Jev selection. `check` revalidates the chosen offer after evaluation. These are mechanical gates; there is no local weighted capability score or task-fit optimizer. [Router](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/router.py); [Jev context](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_context.py); [selector](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/selector.py); [skill contract](../../skills/model-routing/SKILL.md).

When at least two offers remain, Jev receives task outcome, acceptance criteria, relevant facts, authority, failure impact, unknowns, scoped preferences, anonymous quota summaries, and full candidate profiles. A single `Choice` asks for the best offer under policy covering suitability, evidence quality, quota, and premium-capability restraint. Profiles are JSON encoded as option-description strings. The selected alias and full distribution are retained; Jev supplies no prose explanation or estimated worker-success probability. If only one offer remains, the router selects it locally. [Jev context](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_context.py); [selector](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/selector.py); [TypeSafe Choice](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice).

**Judgment beyond gates is possible and useful in principle:** the hard gates cannot distinguish a supplied one-line cache correction from an unresolved cross-process consistency problem when both can run on the same launchers. Jev selected different offers for those two synthetic cases. That demonstrates sensitivity to task facts in one run, not better worker outcomes. The six live trial calls launched no workers, used synthetic tasks and policy, had low confidence on several choices, and did not include every catalog offer. Agent predictions used during that trial are not ground truth. [Trial report](jev-trial-2026-09-20.md); [trial cases](../../skills/model-routing/references/jev-trial-cases.json); [trial runner](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_trial.py).

## Criteria issues to resolve

1. **The Choice combines several judgments in one question.** Task adequacy, wrong-result impact, benchmark transfer, subscription burden, and preferences are all weighed in one option choice. TypeSafe advises atomic questions when a decision has independent factors. The present design preserves Jev's ownership of the final choice, but gives little visibility into *why* one offer won. Compare the present Choice with a preregistered, narrower-question variant on the same tasks; a final Choice conditioned on earlier answers needs a second call because TypeSafe evaluates questions in one request independently. Record whether the added signals improve outcomes enough to justify complexity and latency. [Jev context](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_context.py); [TypeSafe introduction](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction); [TypeSafe Choice](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice).
2. **Benchmark magnitudes are not interchangeable task-success odds.** CursorBench reports performance in Cursor's harness and warns that small score differences may be noise. DeepSWE uses a different task set; the catalog labels its mini-swe-agent and Codex-harness cells as separate scales. AA's Intelligence Index already includes AA-Briefcase at 15%, so reasoning and agentic cells are correlated. The `conservative` deductions for several cells are documented routing heuristics, not measured cross-launcher confidence intervals. Preserve scale and provenance in any future scoring or analysis; test whether removing or separately presenting overlapping cells changes choices. [Catalog](../../skills/model-routing/references/routing-catalog.json); [normalization research](model-routing-score-normalization-2026-09-22.md); [AA methodology](https://artificialanalysis.ai/methodology/intelligence-benchmarking); [CursorBench](https://cursor.com/evals); [DeepSWE](https://deepswe.datacurve.ai/).
3. **Sparse UI and spatial evidence makes task-specific extrapolation likely.** Most offers have `unknown` in those dimensions; only K3 has a UI cell, and K3 and Fable have spatial cells. The built-in preference names Astra for 3D despite its spatial cell being unknown, explicitly as a preference rather than a benchmark claim. Audit 3D and UI selections against actual deliverables before adjusting that preference or converting unknowns to inferred strengths. [Catalog](../../skills/model-routing/references/routing-catalog.json); [normalization research](model-routing-score-normalization-2026-09-22.md).
4. **The probability distribution measures a choice among the *offered* alternatives.** TypeSafe confidence summarizes distribution concentration; neither it nor the winning probability is a chance of task completion. It will also depend on the eligible option set. The router currently records both but has no evidence-backed threshold for them. Do not use a generic confidence cutoff or interpret `0.7` as 70% worker success. If a threshold is introduced, fit it by task risk and option set against observed outcomes. [TypeSafe Choice](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives/choice); [TypeSafe confidence](https://docs.typesafe.ai/confidence); [selector](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/selector.py).

## Session-outcome calibration plan

Collect a consented, privacy-minimized evaluation set of real delegated **outcomes**, not whole sessions or raw transcripts. Before routing, freeze a short task rubric: acceptance criteria, failure severity, allowed launchers, required features/context, and acceptable cost or latency. Save a public catalog version/hash, selector instructions version/hash, eligible offer IDs, benchmark-scale coverage, quota status category, selected offer, distribution/confidence, and gate verdict. After work, assess independent artifacts with the frozen rubric: completion, defect or rework, time/turns, quota or metered cost, and whether escalation was needed. Keep private task text and account identities out of shared reports. [Jev context](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_context.py); [selector](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/selector.py); [trial runner](../../skills/model-routing/scripts/jev_trial.py).

Use paired or randomized comparable tasks to compare Jev against an agent-choice baseline and, where feasible, a simple rule baseline. Stratify mechanical edits, novel diagnosis, agentic repo work, UI, spatial work, and high-cost errors; preserve the same gate policy and offer set within each comparison. Report success and quality, cost, latency, abstention, and wrong-route regret with uncertainty intervals. Repeat borderline cases and test task-context ablations. Analyze confidence **as a predictor of route reliability within comparable option sets**, not as a direct worker-success probability. A positive incremental result, especially where task facts change the appropriate capability level, would justify keeping Jev's extra judgment; absent that result, the integration trial alone does not.
