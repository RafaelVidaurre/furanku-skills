# Visual direction

## Build a visual arc from the song

Choose a coherent rendering language from the brand and musical character.
Possible ingredients include photography, captured UI, typography, physical
materials, procedural light, illustration, or 3D. Define the palette, type roles,
light/material treatment, framing, and motion behavior before multiplying scenes.

A problem → release → product proof → payoff → CTA arc is useful when the song
supports it. A different song may need repetition, a montage, or a sustained
performance. Assign a visual purpose to each musical section rather than
illustrating every noun literally.

Vary scale and composition: a tight product detail, a large phrase, a complete
device or object, a spatial arrangement, then a quiet hold. Keep the viewer's
attention on one idea even when several elements support it. Give the strongest
musical moment a distinctive visual treatment and enough calm afterward to read.

**Complete when:** representative frames show a coherent style with intentional
variation and a clear relationship to the music.

## Typography is part of the performance

Compose readable phrases as units. One useful treatment shows the whole short
phrase dimly, then lights each word as sung; another reveals phrase-sized blocks.
Choose deliberately. Word-by-word replacement can become a reading test.

Split long lines into semantic phrases, keep line breaks stable during emphasis,
and size type for the smallest intended viewing surface. Treat brand spelling,
punctuation, and sung versus displayed URLs as editorial decisions. Use fonts
available to the recipient with suitable redistribution rights; a font installed
on the original creator's computer is not a portable dependency.

An impact word can use condensed weight, a strong silhouette, outline, brief
distortion, or a camera punch. Judge the held frame after the effect: the word
must still read. Build intensity through contrast in scale, rhythm, and texture;
strobes are optional, and a calmer variant may fit the destination better.

Keep entrance, readable hold, and exit intervals explicit. Outgoing and incoming
messages sharing a region must not collide. A crossfade of two sentences is often
unreadable even if each endpoint looks good. Check the incoming product frame too:
text from the previous shot may obscure the actual demonstration.

**Complete when:** phrases read at viewing size, emphasis preserves layout, and
transition frames show a deliberate transfer of attention.

## Make the product proof unmistakable

Match the pictured action to the lyric's claim. Show the product's own interface
around an attachment, result, notification, or export when that context explains
the feature. A full-screen screenshot of another application can imply the wrong
product is being promoted.

Use consistent demo data across the entire sequence. A question, selection, and
result should tell one intelligible story. Crop or enlarge the detail that matters;
a brief shot gives viewers time for one detail, not a full terminal or dashboard.
Stage transformations for legibility within product truth.

**Complete when:** a viewer can connect the claim to visible product behavior
without needing to read incidental interface text.

## Polish the material, not just the effect count

Diagnose what makes a frame feel unfinished: weak hierarchy, flat light, rough
edges, low-resolution UI, inconsistent perspective, arbitrary easing, or competing
styles. Fix that cause before adding another overlay.

For a luminous look, shaders can give glow a soft core, graded falloff, depth,
subtle chromatic edges, and controlled texture. For a graphic look, clean vector
shapes may be exactly right. Either way, match material and light across the
frame; a flat decorative shape beside lit UI reads as cheap.

Use grain, bloom, halation, flare, and chromatic displacement selectively. Apply
finishing after composition, then recheck type and product detail in the encoded
copy. Grain and fine text can degrade under compression. Avoid stacking large
blurred layers when a small texture or procedural background produces the look
more reliably.

When literal illustrative scenery fails, choose between properly rebuilding it
and communicating the idea with type, color, and light.

**Complete when:** the finished excerpt feels consistent at playback speed and
its texture and effects leave the message and product legible.

## Translate critique into a targeted revision

| Note | Inspect and change |
| --- | --- |
| Too much to read | Competing messages, phrase length, UI crop, hold duration |
| Not enough variety | Repeated scale/layout/motion; revise the shot rhythm |
| Needs more intensity | The musical peak's type, scale, attack, and contrast to adjacent shots |
| Looks cheap | Material/light consistency, edges, texture, perspective, stock-looking motifs |
| UI looks grainy | Original capture resolution, scaling, codec generations, final compression |
| Transition feels wrong | Every frame around the cut; text lifetime and motion continuity |
| Wrong product shown | Context around the demonstrated output and the claim being made |

**Complete when:** the revised excerpt addresses the specific note and adjacent
shots still work; technical checks and creative approval remain separate.
